Does water look rusty at first, or does pressure drop at multiple taps?

Repiping may be worth investigating if a home has deteriorating galvanized supply lines, recurring leaks, rusty first-draw water, or pressure that drops when several taps run. The right scope can range from one bathroom or addition to replacing supply piping throughout the house. Are you planning a renovation, or is a toilet or faucet not working properly?

A fixture update may involve more than choosing a faucet or toilet: shut-offs, supply connections, drainage, clearances, and the condition of existing surfaces can all affect installation. For a Stratford renovation, photograph the current fixture connections and note what is staying, such as a clawfoot tub or period tile. For a basement suite or kitchen alteration, ask how drains, vents, and any required backflow protection change the rough-in scope. Has your water use increased without an obvious leak?

A rising water bill, unexplained dampness, or a sound of running water can justify checking for a leak, even when the source is not visible. Water may travel along a wall or beneath a floor before a stain appears, so describe when symptoms began and which fixtures or areas are affected. For a Stratford property, photographs of damp spots, meter readings, and accessible pipes can help scope investigation. Ask whether pressure isolation or acoustic listening is appropriate before openings are made.
